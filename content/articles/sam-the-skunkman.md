**Sam the Skunkman**, born **David Watson**, was an American cannabis breeder and seed bank founder widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of cannabis genetics. Working first in Santa Cruz County, California, in the 1970s and later from Amsterdam, he created **Skunk #1**, a stabilized three-way hybrid whose genetic fingerprint is now found in a large majority of the hybrid cannabis strains grown and sold today, and he founded some of the first commercial cannabis seed companies in the world.[^1][^2] He died on January 27, 2025, at the age of 76.[^3]

## Breeding Skunk #1 in Santa Cruz

Sam's breeding career began in the early 1970s in the hills around Santa Cruz County, California, where he worked with an underground collective known as **Sacred Seeds**.[^1] Sacred Seeds was working with three pure landrace genetics: a hardy Afghan indica out of the Hindu Kush region, prized for its resilience and resin production; the tall, soaring sativa Colombian Gold; and the legendary Mexican sativa Acapulco Gold.[^1]

Sam's initial cross paired the Afghan indica with Colombian Gold, but the resulting plant flowered too late to be practical, so he crossed it again with Acapulco Gold to shorten the flowering period while keeping the plant easy to grow indoors, in greenhouses, or outdoors in Southern California's climate.[^2][^1] According to Sam, the plant that became Skunk #1 was only one of about thirty hybrids he grew and tested from that breeding program, using the same male plant to pollinate each of thirty different females to produce a set of F2 populations. Most F2 hybrids segregate heavily, with roughly a quarter of offspring resembling each parent and the rest falling in between, which makes them unreliable for stable, repeatable seed production. The line that became Skunk #1 showed unusually little segregation, breeding true to type far more consistently than its siblings, which is what made it viable as a commercial seed product rather than a one-off cross.[^2]

## Sacred Seeds and the first commercial cannabis seed bank

By 1981, Sacred Seeds had stabilized Skunk #1 enough to offer it to the public through its first seed catalog, making it one of the earliest commercially available stabilized cannabis hybrids anywhere and establishing Sacred Seeds as, by many accounts, the first commercial cannabis seed bank in the United States.[^1][^2] The operation did not last long in America: in 1982, police raided Sacred Seeds and shut it down. Sam left the country for the Netherlands, reportedly carrying a very large quantity of seeds with him.[^1]

Sam first visited Holland in 1984 and relocated there permanently the following spring, arriving at Schiphol Airport in 1985 and settling with his partner, Diana.[^4] At the time, many Dutch growers were producing low-quality outdoor cannabis, commonly nicknamed "mean green," that struggled against the country's climate and against imported hashish and cannabis of inconsistent potency. Sam bluntly told Dutch growers he met that outdoor cultivation in Holland was a losing proposition and urged them to move into greenhouses instead, advice that reshaped Dutch cultivation practice going forward.[^4]

Operating under the name **Cultivators Choice**, Sam began sharing and selling his genetics, including Skunk #1, with the small number of Dutch seed companies then active, among them Nevil Schoenmakers' The Seed Bank of Holland, the Super Sativa Seed Club, and the Lowlands Weed Company.[^1][^4] According to Sam, Lowlands had the right spirit but not the strongest genetics, while The Seed Bank of Holland and Super Sativa Seed Club built much of their early catalogs around genetics acquired from him and other American breeders.[^4] He went on to co-found the **Hortilab Seed Bank** in the Netherlands and, with his lifelong friend, the botanist and author Robert Connell Clarke, co-founded the **Legends of Hashish** dinner and competition, held each November during the Cannabis Cup.[^1]

Beyond Skunk #1, Sam bred or popularized several other influential lines. He was given Haze genetics by the group known as the Haze Brothers, with permission to reproduce and sell the seeds, and received Afghani #1 from Mel Frank, a partner of the cannabis writer Ed Rosenthal, with similar permission.[^4] He also worked extensively with Durban Poison, a South African sativa whose seeds Ed Rosenthal originally collected from the Transkei Coffeeshop in Amsterdam; Sam found the line he was given was partially intersexed and spent four to five years selecting stable plants in California before growing more than twenty thousand Durban Poison plants outdoors in Holland.[^4]

## Skunk #1's legacy

Skunk #1 achieved mainstream recognition when it won the inaugural High Times Cannabis Cup in 1988, beating entries from the Super Sativa Seed Club, the Sensi Seed Club, and the Seed Bank of Holland.[^4] Its genetic influence has since spread through the cannabis industry to a remarkable degree: breeders estimate Skunk #1 genetics appear somewhere in the ancestry of a large majority of commercial hybrid strains sold today, from Cheese to Super Skunk and countless others.[^1] In the United Kingdom in particular, "skunk" became a generic slang term for any strong, potent cannabis, regardless of whether the plant in question has any real genetic connection to Sam's original line.[^1]

Sam himself argued that the spread of high-quality seeds and clones around the world, helped along by the adoption of artificial grow lights, effectively made the war on drugs unwinnable long before governments were willing to admit it, since premium genetics could no longer be contained within any single country's enforcement regime.[^4]

## Personality and later life

Despite his outsized influence, Sam largely avoided the public eye. He gave few interviews, allowed very few photographs of himself to be published, and preferred to remain, in the words of one obituary, "an ex-pat in Amsterdam" working mostly out of the spotlight.[^3] He remained close friends with Jair Velleman, founder and curator of the World of Cannabis museum, and continued to be a well-known if reclusive presence in the Amsterdam cannabis and hashish scene for the rest of his life.[^3]

## Death

Sam the Skunkman died on January 27, 2025, at the age of 76, in the Netherlands, the country that had been his home for four decades.[^3] By coincidence, his death fell on the same date, seven years apart, as that of the American medical marijuana activist Dennis Peron.[^3] His passing prompted tributes across the cannabis industry, with commentators describing him as, by many accounts, the single most influential cannabis breeder in history.[^1][^3]
